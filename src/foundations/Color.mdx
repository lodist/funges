# Color palette

Every color in the application comes from a **semantic token**, never from a
raw value. A token says what a color is _for_ — a card surface, a focus ring,
a danger fill — so a screen specified in this vocabulary keeps working when the
palette is retuned underneath it.

Use the toolbar **Theme** control to see the dark values. There is one set of
token names; only what they resolve to changes.

## Semantic pairs

Most tokens come in pairs: a surface and the foreground meant to sit on it.
Pick the pair, not the two halves separately — the pairing is what carries the
contrast guarantee.

## Lines and focus

These paint a line, not a surface, so they have no foreground partner.
`--ring` is non-text, which means it answers to WCAG's 3:1 non-text contrast
floor rather than the 4.5:1 body-text one.

<Story of={ColorStories.LineAndFocusTokens} />

## The brand scale

One hue, seven steps. Severity is depth, not hue — the scale carries emphasis
and weight, never a change of colour. Danger is the single exception:
`--destructive` is hue 28, the fly agaric's red, and no other token may use it.
The only other colour outside hues 150 and 90 is the map's score ramp, which
warning chrome borrows literally rather than inventing an amber of its own.

Steps `700` and `900` are the text-safe ones. Steps `500` and `600` are fills:
using them as text color fails body-text contrast.

## Status

Flat across both themes — a status color that shifted with the theme would
stop being recognisable. `--status-warning` is a solid fill bright enough that
text on it needs `--status-warning-text`, not `--foreground`.
